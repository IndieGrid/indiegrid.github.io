How to Track Freelance Invoices in Google Sheets (Free Template)

Published 6 November 2026 by IndieGrid

If you freelance, unpaid and half-paid invoices are where cash quietly disappears. You do not need accounting software on day one. A clean Google Sheet — with one row per invoice, clear statuses, and a running total of what you are owed — is enough to keep money moving.

This guide shows a simple system you can set up in under an hour. At the end you can grab a free starter template, or the full tracker if you want invoices, expenses, and a dashboard in one file.

Why freelancers lose track of invoices

Most freelancers start with a folder of PDFs and a mental note of who paid. That breaks as soon as you have more than a handful of clients. Common failure modes:

  1. No single source of truth — invoices live in email, Drive, and bank statements.
  2. Status is vague — "sent" vs "viewed" vs "overdue" are mixed together.
  3. Tax set-aside is guessed — you see a bank balance and treat it as profit (a separate habit from invoice tracking).
  4. Follow-ups stop — overdue invoices sit because chasing feels awkward without a list.

A spreadsheet fixes the first three. The fourth needs a weekly habit (10 minutes every Friday).

The minimum viable invoice tracker

Create a Google Sheet with one tab named Invoices. Use these columns:

Column Example Why it matters
Invoice # INV-2026-014 Unique ID for disputes and bookkeeping
Client Acme Studio Who owes you
Project / description Landing page redesign Context if a client has many jobs
Issue date 2026-11-01 When you sent it
Due date 2026-11-15 For overdue logic
Amount (ex-VAT) 800 Your fee before tax
VAT / tax 160 If you charge VAT or sales tax
Total 960 What the client must pay
Currency EUR Important if you invoice in more than one currency
Status Sent / Paid / Overdue / Partial Your pipeline
Paid date 2026-11-12 Closes the loop
Paid amount 960 Catches partial payments
Notes Wire ref ABC Anything future-you will need

Status rules that work:

  • Draft — not sent yet
  • Sent — client has it; waiting
  • Partial — paid some, not all
  • Paid — fully settled
  • Overdue — past due date and not Paid
  • Written off — you stopped chasing (rare; keep for honesty)

Do not invent ten statuses. Six is plenty.

Formulas that do the boring work

Assume row 2 is your first data row, amounts in column H (Total), status in column J, due date in column E.

Outstanding balance (what you are owed):

=SUMIFS(H:H, J:J, "Sent") + SUMIFS(H:H, J:J, "Overdue") + SUMIFS(H:H, J:J, "Partial")

Paid this month (if paid date is column K and paid amount is L):

=SUMIFS(L:L, K:K, ">="&EOMONTH(TODAY(),-1)+1, K:K, "<="&EOMONTH(TODAY(),0))

Auto-flag overdue (put in a helper column, or use conditional formatting):

=AND(E2<TODAY(), J2<>"Paid", J2<>"Written off", J2<>"Draft")

In Google Sheets, select the Status column → Format → Conditional formatting → Custom formula, and colour Overdue rows red so they jump out on Friday reviews.

Unique invoice numbers: keep a small Settings tab with Next invoice # in cell B1. After each invoice, increment it. Or use:

="INV-" & TEXT(TODAY(),"yyyy") & "-" & TEXT(COUNTA(A$2:A2)+13,"000")

Pick one scheme and stick to it.

A weekly routine that actually gets you paid

  1. Monday (5 min): Add any new invoices from the weekend. Mark Sent.
  2. Wednesday (5 min): Check bank / Stripe / PayPal. Update Paid and Paid date.
  3. Friday (10 min): Filter Status = Sent or Overdue. Email anyone past due with a polite nudge and the invoice number.
  4. Month-end (20 min): Export the sheet (File → Download → CSV) for your accountant or tax folder.

Chasing is easier when the sheet tells you exactly who and how much. Soft wording works: “Hi — checking in on INV-2026-014 (€960), due 15 Nov. Happy to resend the PDF if useful.”

Separate “money in the bank” from “money you can spend”

Freelancers often overspend after a big payment. This section is a manual habit, not a feature of our templates. If you want it, add your own note or a tiny Cash rules tab with three numbers:

  • Tax set-aside % — e.g. 20–30% of profit reserved for tax
  • Buffer — e.g. one month of fixed costs
  • Spendable — bank balance − tax set-aside − buffer − unpaid supplier bills

Even a rough version stops the “I just got paid, so I can buy the laptop” mistake. Keep that cash-rule math in a separate note or Settings cell if you want — our templates do not compute a tax pot or safe-to-spend number. Google Sheets vs Excel for freelancers

Use Google Sheets if you want access from any device, easy sharing with an accountant, and automatic saving. Use Excel if you prefer offline work or heavier Power Query later. Both can run the same structure. Our templates ship in both formats so you are not locked in.

Tips specific to Sheets:

  • Turn on File → Version history before big cleanups.
  • Protect the header row (Data → Protect sheets and ranges) so you do not sort the headers away.
  • Use Data → Data validation on Status with a fixed list so typos do not break SUMIFS.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Editing paid invoices instead of adding credit notes — keep history; add a new row for adjustments.
  • Mixing personal and business expenses in the invoice sheet — use a separate Expenses tab.
  • Ignoring currency — if you invoice in USD and EUR, track currency per row and convert for dashboards.
  • No due dates — without them you cannot automate Overdue.
  • Skipping invoice numbers — banks and clients ask for them; gaps are fine, duplicates are not.

Quick checklist

  • One Invoices tab with status + due date
  • Outstanding total on a Dashboard or top row
  • Friday overdue review on the calendar
  • Your own tax-set-aside rule written down somewhere you will see it (not built into the template)
  • Backup CSV at month-end

Do those five and you are ahead of most freelancers who “will organise the books later.” Later is how unpaid invoices turn into bad debt.
