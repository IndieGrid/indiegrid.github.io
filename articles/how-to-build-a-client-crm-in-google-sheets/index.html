How to Build a Simple Client CRM in Google Sheets (Free Approach + Template)

Published 6 November 2026 by IndieGrid

You do not need Salesforce to stop dropping leads. If you are a solo freelancer or a tiny studio, a Google Sheet used as a lightweight CRM — one row per opportunity, clear stages, next action dates — will beat a messy inbox every time.

This guide walks through a practical pipeline you can build yourself, then points to a ready-made Client CRM template when you want weighted forecasts and follow-up alerts without reinventing formulas.

What a freelancer CRM actually needs

Enterprise CRMs optimise for teams, reporting hierarchies, and integrations. You need four things:

  1. Who — client or lead name and contact
  2. Where they are — stage in your sales process
  3. How big — deal value and close probability
  4. What next — the next action and when it is due

Anything else is optional until those four are reliable.

Design your stages before you open Sheets

Write your real process, not an aspirational one. A typical freelance pipeline:

Stage Meaning Rough win rate*
Lead Heard of you; not qualified 10%
Qualified Budget + need + timeline exist 25%
Proposal sent Quote or SOW is out 40%
Negotiation Scope or price discussion 60%
Won Signed / deposit paid 100%
Lost Explicit no or ghosted after N days 0%

*Win rates are starting guesses. After 20+ deals, replace them with your own averages.

Keep Lost as a stage (or a status). Hiding losses makes your forecast lie.

Sheet structure

Create a workbook with three tabs: Pipeline, Clients, Dashboard.

Pipeline tab (one row = one opportunity)

Column Example
Deal ID D-042
Client Northwind Co
Contact email sam@northwind.example
Source Referral / LinkedIn / Website
Stage Proposal sent
Value 2500
Currency EUR
Probability % 40
Weighted value =Value * Probability%
Next action Send revised timeline
Next action date 2026-11-10
Owner You
Created 2026-10-28
Closed date (blank until Won/Lost)
Notes Wants March start

Weighted value is the honest forecast number: a €10,000 deal at 20% counts as €2,000 toward this month’s expectation, not €10,000.

Clients tab (one row = one organisation or person)

Keep company details, default rate, timezone, and “how they like to work” notes here. Link to Pipeline by client name (or a Client ID). Separating clients from deals lets one client have multiple projects over time.

Dashboard tab

Pull metrics with COUNTIF / SUMIF:

  • Open deals by stage
  • Total weighted pipeline
  • Deals with next action date ≤ today (overdue follow-ups)
  • Won revenue this month

Example — overdue follow-ups count (Next action date in column K, Stage in E):

=COUNTIFS(K:K, "<="&TODAY(), E:E, "<>Won", E:E, "<>Lost")

That single number is your daily to-do list.

Habits that make a sheet CRM work

Daily (3 minutes): Open Dashboard. Clear anything with a next action date of today or earlier. Update Stage when something moves. Never leave a deal without a next action unless it is Won or Lost.

Weekly (15 minutes): Review every open deal. If nothing happened in 14 days, either schedule a nudge or mark Lost. Stale “maybe” deals inflate your weighted pipeline and your mood.

Monthly (20 minutes): Compare forecast (sum of weighted values you expected to close) vs actual Won revenue. Adjust stage probabilities. Export a CSV backup.

Lead sources worth tracking

Add a Source column and stick to a short list: Referral, Warm intro, Website, Marketplace, Content, Cold outreach, Other. After a quarter you will see which sources produce Won deals — not just noisy leads. That is how you decide where to spend scarce marketing time.

Avoid vanity: a hundred LinkedIn “interested” messages that never reach Qualified are not a pipeline.

Connecting CRM to invoicing

When a deal hits Won:

  1. Create the project folder / kickoff checklist.
  2. Issue invoice #1 (deposit or first milestone) in your invoice tracker.
  3. Copy Deal ID into the invoice Notes so accounting and sales stay linked.

If you already use the Freelancer Invoice + Money Tracker, treat the CRM as the “before money” system and the invoice file as the “after signature” system. Do not try to force both into one overloaded tab.

Privacy and professionalism (especially in the EU)

A CRM holds personal data (names, emails). Keep access limited (your Google account, maybe your accountant). Do not paste the sheet into public Slack channels. If you work with EU clients, know why you store each contact and delete old Lost leads you will never contact again. A spreadsheet is still a filing system under GDPR — keep it tidy.

When to graduate from a sheet

Move to a dedicated CRM when you have a team sharing the same pipeline, need email sequences, or close more deals than you can scroll. Until then, Sheets wins on cost, speed, and ownership of your data (File → Download anytime).

One-page setup checklist

  • Stages written down with rough probabilities
  • Pipeline tab with Value, Probability, Weighted value, Next action date
  • Dashboard showing overdue follow-ups
  • Weekly stale-deal review on the calendar
  • Won deals flow into your invoice tracker

Do this and your “I forgot to follow up” rate drops sharply — usually within the first two weeks.
