How the gap is calculated

A set-aside calculator answers a narrower question than a tax return. For the income you are looking at, how much should already be in a separate reserve, and how far is the reserve from that rule?

The percentage is one you type. This page does not store a country, a tax bracket, a VAT scheme, or a filing deadline. Two people with the same revenue can owe different amounts, and the amount can change when life changes. What most freelancers need first is a habit with a visible gap: choose a percentage, move that share when money arrives, and see whether the reserve is behind, level, or ahead.

Income received is the money that arrived in the period you pick: this month, this quarter, or this year. The set-aside percent is the share of that income you have decided to reserve. Already set aside is cash you have actually moved, not cash you planned to move. The period does not change the multiplication. It names the window the gap belongs to, so a quarterly shortfall is not mistaken for a yearly one.

The page uses two lines and nothing hidden behind them.

should have = income × (percent ÷ 100)
gap = should have − already set aside

A positive gap means the reserve is short of the rule you chose. A negative gap means you have moved more than that rule required. Zero means the transfers match the percentage. The per-payment rule is the same percentage applied to the next amount a client pays. If the rule is 25% and a 2,000 payment clears, 500 moves the same day and gets logged. Repeating that is what keeps the gap from opening again after you have just closed it.

Worked example

This quarter you received 10,000. You chose 25%. The reserve holds 1,800.

should have = 10,000 × 0.25 = 2,500
gap = 2,500 − 1,800 = 700

You are 700 short for the quarter. You can transfer 700 now if you want the quarter to land on zero, and you still move 25% of every later payment. Neither transfer is a tax payment to an authority. It is money shifted into a pot you do not spend on ordinary costs.

The form rejects a percentage above 100, because that is no longer a share of the income you entered. It will not lower the percentage because you had expenses, and it will not tell you a rate for where you live. If you later agree a different percentage with an advisor, type the new number. The previous gap was still the right reading of the previous rule.
